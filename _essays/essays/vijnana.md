*श्रुतेः किङ्करतामेति*(१) वाङ्मनः कायकर्मसु ॥३९॥

Commentary of Jñanottama:
वृत्तानुकीर्त्तनपूर्वकं वत्तिष्यमाणाविद्यान्वयग्रन्थस्य तात्पर्यमाह र्माज्ञानेति । अज्ञानसमुत्थत्वात्कर्माज्ञाननिवर्त्तकं न भवतीत्युक्तम् । इर नीमध्यासपूर्वककर्माधिकारहेतुत्वाद्धि कर्मणामज्ञाननिवर्त्तकत्वं नारु ति युक्त्यन्तरप्रदर्शनाय श्लोकारम्भ इत्यर्थः । प्रदर्शयिष्यामीत्यत आर्हो प्रदर्शयिष्यामीत्यतो हेतोराह एतद्धमाहेति यावत् । ब्राह्मण्याद्यात्म इति । वर्णाश्रमवयोवस्थाविशेषवति देहे । ना पुरुष आत्मेतिभाव लात्वा समादायेत्यविद्यान्वयो दर्शितः । सांप्रतं तद्धीनं कर्तृत्वभोः त्वादिसंसारान्वयं दर्शयति श्रुतेरिति । किङ्करतां तन्नियोगात्तदुः कारिताम् । ततश्च कर्मणामविद्याविनाभूतत्वान्नाविद्यानिवर्त्तकत्वां त्यर्थः ॥ ३९ ॥

Sureśvara:
यस्मात्कर्माज्ञानसमुत्थमेव तस्मात्तद्व्यावृत्तौ निवर्तत इत्युच्यते-

वर्तमानः श्रुतेर्मूर्ध्नि (२) *नैव स्याद्वेदकिंकरः* ॥४०॥

In srishTa-drishTi-vAda, suppose I am in a room and there is pot in other room which I cannot see. Then, that pot-avachchhinna-chaitanya is accepted to be covered by avasthA-ajnAna.

mUla-ajnAna is defined as शुद्ध-ब्रह्म-आवरक-अज्ञानम्

[2/20/26, 7:21:01 AM] Sudhanshu Shekhar: *MithyA-jnAa is not the cover-or of Brahman*

The opponent (O) is a bheda-abheda-vAdi. His stand is that there is no need for a bhAvarUpa-ajnAna which is different from agrahNa, mithyA-jnAna and its samskAra. Rather, bhrAnti i.e. mithyA-jnAna itself can be called ajnAna.

The siddhAntI (S) asks as to what exactly is this bhrAnti which can be called ajnAna and is capable of covering the illumination of Brahma-swarUpa. O says that “Atma-buddhi in anAtmA starting from ahamkAra till body” in the form of “अहं मनुष्यः” is the bhrAnti which is ajnAna and is capable of covering the illumination of Brahman.

S says that it is not possible to prove that “अहं मनुष्यः” is a bhrAnti. This is on account of:

(a)	non-bhrAnti-hood of “खण्डो गौः”; and 
(b)	non-difference between “अहं मनुष्यः” and “खण्डो गौः”.

*[खण्ड: refer to a cow with broken horns. मुण्डः refers to a cow which naturally has no horns.]*

*O: But there is the availability of cognition in the form “न अहं मनुष्यः, अहं ब्रह्म अस्मि”. Hence, there is a difference of “अहं मनुष्यः” vis-a-vis “खण्डो गौः”. Your claim in (b) above regarding the non-difference of these two cognitions is incorrect.* 

S: Not so. Because there is the availability of cognition in the form of  “न अयं खण्डो गौः, अयन्तु  मुण्डः”. This cognition arises when we see a मुण्डः. So, just as there is abheda of गोत्व with खण्ड and मुण्ड, similarly let there be abheda of AtmA with sharIra and Brahman. Why should “अहं मनुष्यः” be a bhrAnti. “अहं मनुष्यः” will be as valid as  “खण्डो गौः”.

*O: But “अहं मनुष्यः” is like “इदं रजतम्”. Just as there is nishedha of rajata in pratipanna-upAdhi idam, similarly there is nishedha of manushyatva in pratipanna-upAdhi AtmA by the cognition (from shAstra) “नाहं मनुष्यः”.*

S: Not so. When we see a खण्ड, then there is tAdAtmya of खण्ड in गोत्व. And then when we see another cow which is मुण्ड, then there is nishedha of खण्ड in the same गोत्व because we say “न अयं खण्डो गौः, अयन्तु  मुण्डः”. So, just as there is nishedha of rajata in pratipanna-upAdhi idam, similarly there is nishedha of खण्ड in pratipanna-upAdhi गोत्व. So, there will arise the contingency of bhrAnti-hood of even “खण्डो गौः” which you don’t admit. 

*O: But खण्ड is negated in मुण्ड and not in गोत्व.*

S: Not so. Because खण्ड is never imposed in मुण्ड. So, where is the need to negate it? खण्ड is imposed in गोत्व. And there itself is it negated (when मुण्ड is seen).

*O: But pratipanna-upAdhi is not गोत्व. It is गोत्व-delimited-by-खण्ड. And there खण्ड is never negated. खण्ड is negated in गोत्व-delimited-by-मुण्ड. So, “खण्डो गौः” cannot be equated with “इदं रजतम्”.*

S: Well. Then in “अहं मनुष्यः” also, the pratipanna-upAdhi is आत्मा-delimited-by-मनुष्यत्व. And there manushyatva is never negated. Manushyatva is negated in आत्मा-delimited-by-ब्रह्मत्व. That is not the pratipanna-upAdhi. So, it will not be analogous to “idam rajatam”.

*O: But there is continuance of transactions in the form of “खण्डो गौः”. But there is no continuance of transaction in the form of “idam rajatam”. So, there is a divergence.*

S: Here also there is continuance of transaction in the form of “अहं मनुष्यः” because even in mOksha, due to sarvAtma-bhAva, there will be identification with manushya-body.

*O: But there are only five instances of bheda-abheda namely jAti-vyakti, guNa-guNI, kArya-kAraNa,visheshya-visheshaNa and avayava-avayavI. Since deha-AtmA-abheda is not one of these five, hence it is bhrAnti.*

S: Not so. Admittedly, these five are not required to be present together for bheda-abheda. They are individually capable. And if they are individually capable, then instead of being five in number, let them be six in number. Please add even deha-AtmA-bheda-abheda i.e. sharIra-sharIrI-bheda-abheda. What is the obstacle? There is no obstacle. And if you don’t accept the bheda-abheda of deha-AtmA without any justifiable reason, then you are prevented from accepting the bheda-abheda in rest five as well. 

Thus, it transpires that your claim of “अहं मनुष्यः” cannot be proved to be a case of bhrAnti at all. And since it cannot be proved so, it cannot be called ajnAna and hence your claim of it capable of covering Brahman is equally without merit.

Śańkara says that this world of men is to be won or attained through the son alone as means, and by no other rite, nor meditation. With regard to the world of the Manes, he says that it is "to be won through rites alone such as the agnihotra and not through either the son or meditation." Again, with regard to the world of the gods, he says that it is to be won "through meditation and not through either the son or rites."

Sureśvara objects to the addition of the word "eva" to "karmaṇā" and "vidyayā” which Śankara makes in the explanation which he gives in his commentary. It is but proper to say, observes Sureśvara, that the world of the gods is to be won through meditation and that the world of the Manes through rites, because there is the scriptural statement that meditation and rites are the means. But it is wrong to say by adding "eva" to "karmaṇā" and "vidyayā" that the world of the Manes is to be won through rites alone and that the world of gods through meditation alone, since they can be attained through other means also. The addition of "eva" to the word "putrena," which we find in the text itself, is justified, since the world of men is to be won through the son alone and not through any other means.  

Sureśvara not only criticises Sankara's interpretation but also emphatically declares that of the two interpretations his own interpretation is quite sound and should be accepted and not that of Sankara which is defective.

 Translation:
Sureśvara's Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad bhāṣya Varttika -
This explanation alone is free from defect, while the other is defective.
Therefore this alone is to be accepted, and not the view that is flawed.

Ānandagiri's commentary:
The principle that “where the restrictive particle is explicitly heard, it applies there and nowhere else” is alone free from defect, since it does not contradict scripture or tradition. By contrast, the interpretation that supplies the restrictive particle in both sentences is defective, since it conflicts with them.

Therefore, the result is stated: this explanation alone is to be accepted by the learned, and not that of the Bhāṣyakāra.

By the phrase “this explanation,” reference is made to the statement “by action,” etc., in which “here” refers to the human world. Given that one interpretation is free from defect while the other is defective, the conclusion follows accordingly: this alone is to be accepted.

भावप्रतियोगी ह्यभाव:।

"For indeed, non-existence (Abhāva) is the counterpart (counter-positive) of existence (Bhāva)."

No one denies the positive nature (or existential reality) of Self-knowledge. Therefore, its counterpart, Ignorance (Avidya), must be a non-existence (Abhava). If, by some strange logic, Avidya is claimed to be a positive entity (Bhava), then Avidya would become indestructible.

न हि भावानां निरन्वयो निरुपाख्यो विनाशः संभवति। Br. Su. 2.2.22

"When any positive entity (Bhava) loses its specific form, even if that form is destroyed, its 'positivity' (Bhavatva) persists and carries over into another form. This makes it available for 'description' (Upakhyana) — meaning it can be described through that new form. This is because the underlying entity is the 'essential nature' (Svarupa) of all its various forms. While a specific form may perish, the essential nature does not.

This implies that if Ignorance (Avidya) were a positive entity, an absolute and trace-less destruction would be impossible for it. Consequently, there would be no opportunity or 'room' for its counterpart — Knowledge (Vidya) — to ever arise."

‘नास्ति विशेषसंज्ञेति’ — अहमसौ, अमुष्यपुत्रः, ममेदं क्षेत्रम्, धनम्, सुखी, दु:खी — इत्येवमादिलक्षणा अविद्याकृतत्वात्, तस्या अविद्यायाश्च ब्रह्मविद्यया निरन्वयतो नाशितत्वात्, कुतो विशेषसंज्ञासम्भवो ब्रह्मविदश्चैतन्यस्वभावावस्थितस्य? शरीरावस्थितस्यापि विशेषसंज्ञा नोपपद्यते। Br. Bh. 2.4.12

"I am stating that 'particularized identification'—such as 'I am this person,' 'I am the son of so-and-so,' 'this field and wealth are mine,' or 'I am happy or sad'—no longer exists [for the realized soul]. This is because such identification is a product of Ignorance (Avidya).

Since that Ignorance has been destroyed root and branch (without a trace) by the Knowledge of Brahman, how could a Knower of Brahman, who is established in their true nature as Pure Consciousness, possess any such particularized identification? Even for a Knower who is still residing within a physical body, the existence of such specific identification is logically untenable."

"In the case of a pot and the knowledge of a pot, there exists a 'triad' (triputi) consisting of the Knower, the Act of Knowing, and the Object Known. Distinct from this, when 'non-existence' (abhava) is the object of knowledge, the 'object' itself is not actually present. Consequently, there is no corresponding mental cognition (buddhi-pratyaya) of such an object either. Only the Knower remains.

Therefore, whether one says, 'The object is a non-existence because knowledge of it did not arise,' or 'Knowledge did not arise because the object is not there'—these are merely two different verbal expressions (shabda-vikalpa) for the same thing."

"Even though both existence (Bhava) and non-existence (Abhava) are objects of knowledge (Jñeya), there is a vast difference (mahadantara) in how they are known. In the knowledge of a physical object, the mind takes on the specific form of that object (Vishayakara-buddhi-pratyaya). However, in the awareness of its absence, no such mental form or thought-wave exists.

Furthermore, the arising or non-arising of this mental form (the thought-wave) is directly dependent on whether the object itself exists or does not exist."

प्रत्ययाप्रत्ययौ हि सद्भावासद्भावयोः कारणम्। Br. Su. 1.3.33

"Conversely, since the perception (attainment) of an object occurs only through the five means of knowledge, [it is concluded that] 'the non-perception of a distinct object that ought to have been accessible to the means of perception is, in itself, the non-existence of that object.'"

उपलब्धिलक्षणप्राप्तस्यानुपलब्धिरेव अभावो वस्त्वन्तरस्य। Br. Su. 2.2.17

"That is to say, we cannot perceive the non-existence of an object in the same way we perceive an object directly in front of us. When a certain object is imagined to be present on a particular substratum (based on some reason), we must first investigate its presence using the appropriate means of knowledge (Pramana). Only when it is not found there [after such an investigation] can we conclude its non-existence."

"This means that we understand non-existence (Abhava) only in relation to existence (Bhava). Whether it is the 'prior non-existence' (Pragabhava) of a pot or its 'destruction' (Pradhvamsabhava), they both occur only in relation to the clay (the material cause). The same applies to 'mutual non-existence' (Anyonyabhava). In this very manner, the absence of Self-knowledge is explained only in relation to Knowledge itself. Since the knowledge of the True Self (Sadatman) is a positive entity, the ignorance that existed prior to attaining it is defined accordingly with reference to Bhava only"

प्राक्सदात्मविज्ञानात्। Ch. Bh. 7.26.1

"Since non-existence (Abhava) possesses no specific attributes of its own, it is merely a conceptual construct (Vikalpa). Non-existence is the counterpart (Pratiyogi) of existence. This means that when existence (of an object) is present, its counterpart, non-existence, cannot be there.

However, just as a single substance like clay or cotton can be differentiated into various forms like 'the being of a pot' or 'the being of a cloth,' similarly, non-existence—which is actually devoid of distinctions (Nirvishesha)—is conceptually divided based on its relationship to actions or qualities, much like physical substances. Even though it is described this way, non-existence does not possess inherent qualities like a blue lotus does. If it truly possessed such specific attributes, it would become a positive existence (Bhava) itself."
